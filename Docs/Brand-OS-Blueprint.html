Brand OS — Brand-to-Launch platform blueprint

The workflow, software architecture and module flows for a platform that carries an agency client from first brief to final handoff, with every deliverable, version and approval connected in one system.

Source: Docs/R&D.mdPrepared 23 Sep 2026Status: architecture proposal

The hard part of running an agency project is not task management. It is keeping briefs, assets, versions, feedback, approvals, dependencies and brand decisions connected across the whole project. Today that information is spread across email, chat and shared drives, versions get confused, and it is often unclear who approved what.

From client brief to final brand launch, every creative decision, asset, revision and approval sits in one connected workflow.

The platform rests on four foundations. Every other module is built around them.

Foundation 1

Workflow engine

Controls how the project moves through stages, including parallel work and approval gates.

Foundation 2

Deliverable + version engine

Controls what is being produced. Work is never overwritten; every revision is a new version.

Foundation 3

Review + approval engine

Controls who can approve what, and records who, what, which version, when and what decision.

Foundation 4

Intelligence layer

Understands how brand, product, assets and project data relate. It sits on top of the system, not underneath it.

01Project workflow

The original workflow was a straight line. Real agency work runs in parallel. Once Brand KT is approved, branding, product and website strategy can start at the same time, and UI/UX and content can run side by side after the shoot. The backend has to model stages as a dependency graph, not a checklist.

02Deliverable lifecycle

The deliverable is the central object of the application. A logo, a moodboard, a homepage design, a product photo set, a QA report: each is a deliverable with a brief, owner, reviewer, approver, files, versions, comments, dependencies, due date, revision count and audit history. Work does not go from Task → Done. It moves through an explicit state machine.

Fig 2 · Deliverable state machine. A revision always creates a new version (V1 → V2), and the new version gets its own review. Earlier versions are kept along with their reviewers, comments, timestamps and approval status.

This record is the project's legal and operational audit trail. Approvals must happen inside the platform. A "👍" on WhatsApp does not count unless an authenticated WhatsApp approval flow is deliberately built.

Revision limits

Each deliverable tracks its revision rounds against the contract (for example 2 / 3 used). When the limit is reached, a further request goes to the PM as a scope change instead of another free loop.

  • Extra revision requested
  • Scope change
  • PM review
  • Approved as paid change, or declined
Status is not approval. A deliverable can be technically complete but not yet approved. Track production status (for example "Development: Completed") separately from approval status (for example "Approval: Waiting for Client").

Workspace · dashboards

The agency project dashboard answers five questions at a glance: where are we (stage and %), what is blocked, what needs my attention, what happens next, and are we on schedule (for example Day 27 / 45, on track). Every screen shows a Next action with who it is waiting on, so no one has to ask "what do I do next?". The client dashboard shows only stage progress and a short list of items that need their approval.

Client Intake

A guided portal replaces collecting information over WhatsApp and email. Seven sections: Brand, Products, Audience, Competitors, Website, Content, Assets. It covers basic details, business model, pricing, distribution, primary and secondary audience, pain points, direct and indirect competitors, and positioning (what makes the brand different, what customers should feel, what the brand must never say).

  • Client invited
  • Questionnaire
  • Asset upload
  • Submit
  • Event: brand.submitted

Brand KT · Brand Intelligence

Brand KT is stored as structured knowledge, not one large document: identity, positioning, audience, competitors, products, pricing, tone, visual language, messaging, keywords, forbidden terms, brand rules and references. AI drafts a brand summary (position, audience, tone, visual direction, what to avoid). A person must confirm it; AI never sets brand rules on its own.

  • Client input
  • AI processing
  • Structured brand profile
  • Creative team review
  • Approved brand knowledge

Product Hub · Product KT

The catalog holds categories, collections, product groups and products. Each product has info, SKU, variants, pricing, inventory, images and videos. Each product also gets a knowledge record: benefits, target customer, key ingredients, approved claims, restrictions, visual direction and shoot requirements. It is the one authoritative record, entered once and reused by shoot, website, marketing, content, ads, email and packaging.

QA Center

Functional, visual, responsive, browser, performance, accessibility, content, SEO, Shopify and brand QA. Each issue records page, type, severity, description, screenshot, environment, assignee, status, comments and resolution.

  • Open
  • Assigned
  • In progress
  • Fixed
  • Retest
  • Passed

Brand QA

Compares the actual output with approved brand rules: correct logo and proportions, approved palette only, approved fonts and hierarchy, photography style, tone, product claims and overall consistency. AI can check automatically, for example "⚠ Heading font mismatch: expected Playfair Display, found Georgia" or "⚠ Claim '100% chemical free' is not in approved claims".

  • AI detected
  • Human review
  • Confirm
  • Resolve

AI findings do not block launch unless a hard rule has been deliberately configured.

Launch Center

Pre-launch checklist covering domain, hosting, analytics, pixel and tracking, SEO, forms, email, payments, shipping, product, inventory, mobile, performance and backup. It rolls up into a launch readiness score (for example 87%, with branding, UI, development and QA passed, and analytics and SEO metadata still flagged).

Handoff Center

Organised packages instead of a Drive folder: brand assets, logo package, guidelines, product assets, photography, videos, website assets, source files, credentials, documentation and training.

  • Handoff package
  • Guided walkthrough
  • Client confirms: assets, website, docs, training, no open issues
  • Final sign-off · Completed

07AI & RAG layer

AI sits on top of reliable workflow data. It understands the project; it is not the project. Every AI output goes through the same governance as human work, and a person can always accept, edit, reject or regenerate it.

Brand AI

Understands brand, tone, positioning, visual identity, guidelines, audience

Creative AI

Drafts moodboards, concepts, copy, creative directions, shot concepts

Product AI

Understands products, SKUs, benefits, pricing, inventory, categories

QA AI

Checks brand compliance, content, visual consistency, product accuracy

Automation AI

Suggests next action, missing dependency, blocked stage, required approval
